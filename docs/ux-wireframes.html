Design system

Two contexts, not two themes. Browse runs light (parchment, drawn from the Mīzān brief's own print palette). Teach runs dark, always — the teacher is in a darkened room and a white phone screen blinds them and distracts students. Dark is not a user preference here; it's a property of the mode.

Three decisions worth defending here.
  • One primary button. Teach is the only filled button on the page. Print, Download and the locale switch are all secondary — if everything is prominent, a teacher at 7:50am reads nothing.
  • The locale switch is on this page, not in global settings. Per the brief: language is chosen once, before teaching, and sets everything downstream. It belongs next to Teach because that's the decision it modifies. Grey out AR when no Arabic sibling row exists yet — don't hide it, or nobody learns the feature exists.
  • Slide thumbnails are clickable. The brief doesn't ask for this, but a teacher previewing at their desk needs to see slide 5 large with its script before deciding how to handle the silence. It's a lightbox over content already on the page — near-zero cost. Drawn next.

Six characters. Codes expire after 3 minutes.

Why board-first, not phone-first. The alternative — teacher generates a code on the phone and types it into the PC — means typing on a keyboard that's often across the room, sticky, or shared. Board-first means the classroom PC only ever needs a bookmark. The tradeoff is that the code must be readable from wherever the teacher stands, which drives the board's 78px type.

Input details that matter: dir="ltr" even in Arabic (codes are Latin base32), autocapitalize on, autocorrect off, inputmode that surfaces letters+digits, and O→0 / I→1 / L→1 normalized server-side so a misread glyph still works.

Next — 6 · A quiet experiment
Eight decisions, each traceable to "2-second glance, one hand, dim room":
  • Script at 20px. Larger than any body text in the browse app. Do not shrink it to fit more on screen — fitting more is not the goal; being readable without focusing is.
  • Stage directions are visually distinct. "Read it, then stop" is an instruction to the teacher, not words to say. Needs a light markup convention in the notes field so it renders differently — worth agreeing with the Mīzān team during authoring, not retrofitting.
  • Next is huge and thumb-reachable; Prev is deliberately smaller. Forward is the 95% action; a mis-tap backward is more disruptive than a missed forward tap.
  • Elapsed time, not a countdown. Shown as 02:30 / 03:00, muted, never turning red. The target comes from Slide#suggested_seconds; elapsed resets on advance; a slide with no target shows elapsed only. The brief wants suggested timing; a countdown that goes red mid-lesson makes a teacher rush a silence that was supposed to be uncomfortable.
  • Slide title and next-slide preview are one line of text each — both from Slide#title (fallback: "Slide N"), never a thumbnail. A teacher needs to know where they are and what's coming, not to see it.
  • End confirms before ending. An in-page Turbo confirm — "End lesson? The board will clear." — never window.confirm (browser dialogs are banned in the companion). An accidental End is otherwise unrecoverable mid-lesson: the pairing code was nulled on pair.
  • The connection indicator is always visible. A teacher must learn a tap didn't land before they're standing in silence facing a stale slide.
  • Screen Wake Lock. navigator.wakeLock.request("screen") while a session is active — otherwise the phone locks after 30 seconds of talking and iOS suspends the websocket. Show the "awake" state so the teacher trusts it, and re-request on visibilitychange, since the lock is dropped whenever the tab is hidden.

The rule that makes these states survivable: the script never disappears. Whatever the network does, the teacher keeps the words — they're rendered server-side into the page at load, not fetched per slide. Even fully offline the phone remains a usable teaching script, which is the same reason the printed sheet exists.

Offline navigation is script-browsing only. "Read ahead" moves the script, never claims to move the board — the board is on the arrow keys now. On reconnect the phone resyncs to the server's current_page (what the class actually saw) with a brief "back on slide N" notice; the phone rejoins the board's reality, not the other way round.

Notice what "Disconnected" does not say: it doesn't say "check your connection." It gives the teacher the next physical action — walk to the PC, use the arrow keys — because they are standing in front of thirty children and cannot debug wifi.
